The Case for Fiber Diversity

Why Six Different Fibers Are Better Than One

Not all dietary fibers are the same. Your gut microbiome is made up of hundreds of species — and research published in Cell Host & Microbe suggests different microbial populations thrive on different fiber substrates. A single-fiber supplement feeds only a narrow slice of your gut ecology.

The Full Science Behind Villora's Formula

🌾
Benefit 01 — Fiber #1

Psyllium Husk: The Gut's Best-Known Ally

Psyllium husk is derived from the seeds of Plantago ovata. When it contacts water, it forms a thick, gel-like mucilage that travels through the digestive tract largely intact. This gel action serves two purposes: it softens stool (helping with constipation) while also bulking loose stool (helping with the other direction). The FDA has authorized a health claim linking psyllium husk consumption with a reduced risk of coronary heart disease when combined with a low-fat diet.

Reference: Dahl WJ, Stewart ML. Position of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics: Health Implications of Dietary Fiber. J Acad Nutr Diet. 2015;115(11):1861-1870.
🌾
Benefit 02 — Fiber #2

Oat Bran: Beta-Glucan and Gut Microbiome Diversity

Oat bran's most notable compound is beta-glucan — a soluble fiber recognized by the FDA and EFSA (European Food Safety Authority) for its cholesterol-lowering properties. In the gut, beta-glucan is fermented by Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium species, generating short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) — particularly butyrate — which nourish colonocytes (the cells lining your colon). This is a direct prebiotic interaction that pairs synergistically with the L. acidophilus in Villora.

Reference: El Khoury D, et al. Beta Glucan: Health Benefits in Obesity and Metabolic Syndrome. J Nutr Metab. 2012;2012:851362.
🌱
Benefit 03 — Fiber #3

Flax Seed: Omega-3, Lignans and Dual-Action Fiber

Flax seed is one of the few plant foods that delivers meaningful amounts of alpha-linolenic acid (ALA, a plant omega-3), dietary lignans (antioxidant compounds) and a mix of both soluble and insoluble fiber. The insoluble fiber provides bulk and promotes regular bowel movements, while the soluble mucilage in flax has mild prebiotic properties. The Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health recognizes flaxseed as a nutrient-dense food that supports digestive and cardiovascular health.

🍎
Benefit 04 — Fiber #4

Apple Pectin: The Prebiotic Fiber From Your Fruit Bowl

Pectin is found in the cell walls of apples and other fruits. As a highly fermentable soluble fiber, it is one of the most effective prebiotic substrates available — it selectively feeds beneficial gut bacteria including Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium species, potentially increasing their populations over time. Pectin also forms a gel in the stomach that slows carbohydrate absorption and contributes to a feeling of fullness after meals, which research associates with reduced post-meal digestive discomfort.

Reference: Holscher HD. Dietary fiber and prebiotics. Gut Microbes. 2017;8(2):172-184.
🌿
Benefit 05 — Fiber #5

Konjac Root (Glucomannan): Nature's Most Viscous Fiber

Konjac root is the source of glucomannan — a water-soluble fiber with an extraordinary ability to absorb water (up to 50 times its weight). When glucomannan enters the stomach, it expands to form a thick gel, slowing gastric emptying and promoting a feeling of fullness. In the colon, it is fermented into SCFAs and acts as a potent prebiotic. The European Food Safety Authority has assessed glucomannan in relation to weight management and normal bowel function.

🍑
Benefit 06 — Fiber #6

Plum Powder: Traditional Gut Remedy, Modern Validation

Plums and prunes have a centuries-long history as digestive aids. The mechanism? A combination of sorbitol (a naturally occurring sugar alcohol with mild osmotic laxative effects), insoluble fiber, phenolic compounds, and chlorogenic acids that together support gut motility. A 2011 clinical trial published in Alimentary Pharmacology & Therapeutics found that dried plum consumption was more effective than psyllium for mild constipation in adults. In Villora, plum powder complements the other five fibers with this distinct motility-supporting mechanism.

🌰
Benefit 07 — Cleanse

Green Black Walnut Hull: Traditional Digestive Cleanser

The outer hull of the black walnut (Juglans nigra), harvested while still green, contains juglone, tannins and other polyphenolic compounds. In traditional herbal medicine across North America, black walnut hull has long been used as a digestive cleanser and intestinal support agent. It is worth noting that Villora's formula contains walnut — a tree nut — and is therefore not suitable for individuals with tree nut allergies. Villora is otherwise vegan and non-GMO.

Benefit 08 — Repair

Lactobacillus acidophilus (1.5B CFU): The Gut's Resident Protector

Lactobacillus acidophilus is one of the most studied probiotic strains in clinical research. It colonizes the small intestine and produces lactic acid, which creates an environment less hospitable to pathogenic bacteria. L. acidophilus has been associated in research with improved lactose digestion, support for healthy bowel function and immune modulation at the gut level. Villora guarantees 1.5 billion CFU at the time of manufacture. The fiber blend in Villora acts as a prebiotic substrate for L. acidophilus — making the combination a true synbiotic (probiotic + prebiotic together). Learn more at the National Center for Biotechnology Information's probiotic resource.
